Architectural Deep Dive: Why BDH Outperforms LSTM

Physical and inductive bias reasons why parallel multi-scale convolutions and temporal attention excel on SUIT chromospheric flare precursors.

Baby Dragon Hatchling (BDH)
Multi-Scale Temporal CNN
1
Multi-Scale Temporal Convolutions (k=3, 7, 15)
Chromospheric flare precursors exhibit both sudden impulsive flash spikes (~2–4 minutes) and prolonged thermal energy ramp-up (~15–30 minutes). BDH captures these multiple temporal scales concurrently via parallel kernel banks.
2
Dilated Gated Residual Blocks (d=1, 2, 4)
Cascaded exponential dilations expand the temporal receptive field to span the full 80-frame history window (~100 min) without pooling, retaining exact frame-by-frame timing of explosive chromospheric brightenings.
3
Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Channel Attention
Dynamically recalibrates feature importance across the 17 physical and statistical channels (e.g., boosting kurtosis and maximum intensity flux over mean flux).
4
Learned Temporal Attention Pooling
A learned query attends dynamically to pre-flare precursor frames while down-weighting quiet solar granulation background, suppressing false alarms.
LSTM Baseline
Recurrent Baseline
1
Sequential Recurrent State Updating
LSTMs process the 80-frame sequence sequentially step-by-step. Hidden state transitions are prone to gradient decay and noise accumulation over multi-hour observation sequences.
2
Single Temporal Granularity
Standard recurrent gates lack multi-scale receptive fields, making it difficult to simultaneously isolate sudden micro-flares and slow magnetic flux emergence.
3
High False Alarm Susceptibility
To achieve high recall on rare events, LSTM shifts its decision boundary so far that it misclassifies 50 quiet observation sequences as flares, limiting operational deployment.
4
Lack of Explicit Temporal Attention
Without an attention mechanism, the final hidden state $\mathbf{h}_T$ can be diluted by recent quiet frames right before an eruptive flux rope instability.
